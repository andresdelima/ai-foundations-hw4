# Design: "The Old Campus Shop Window" (Problem 10)

**Concept:** the site should feel like walking past a real shop on Broadway in New Haven: a lit window display, felt pennants, varsity letters, hang tags, and a Bulldog behind the counter. Every visual idea is a physical object from a college merch shop, so the brand is recognizable within seconds and the site doesn't read as a generic template.

Files: `frontend/src/theme.css` (the whole design system, loaded after `index.css`), `components/Brand.tsx` (mascot, stamp seal, marquee, scroll reveal), plus `Home.tsx`, `NavBar.tsx`, `Footer.tsx`, `ProductCard.tsx`, `ProductDetail.tsx`, and `ChatWidget.tsx`.

| Area | What changed | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts** | Three roles: **Fraunces** (a soft collegiate serif) for headlines and product names; **Graduate** (varsity block letters) for labels, sizes, badges, and slogans; **Open Sans** (the real yalebulldogblue.com body font) for reading. | The serif says "Yale" and premium; the varsity face says "team gear". Keeping Open Sans for body text keeps long copy easy to read. Clear roles make the hierarchy (what is it → what does it cost → details) readable at a glance. |
| **Color** | Yale blue `#00356b` ink on warm **paper cream** with a faint dot texture; deep navy for the header and footer; one **coral** accent used only for urgency (low stock, the headline swoosh, marquee stars). | Cream instead of stark white feels like a physical shop and makes the navy products stand out. Keeping coral rare means it always means "pay attention" (e.g. the pulsing **Low stock** badge), which nudges a purchase decision. |
| **Mascot** | A custom SVG **Bulldog mark** (Handsome Dan-style, with a "Y" collar) used as the logo, the chat launcher, the chat avatar, and the window stamp. | One friendly character ties the brand to the chat helper. Shoppers are more likely to ask a "person" a question, and every question answered is a sale unblocked. |
| **Hero / shop window** | An arched shop window with real products **hanging on a clothesline** (wooden pins, gentle sway, price hang tags), a "Now in the window" sign, a rotating **"Officially licensed · Made in New Haven" rubber-stamp seal**, a hand-drawn coral underline drawing itself under *Bulldog*, and proof numbers (102 styles · 11 colleges · 24/7 help). | The first screen shows actual products with prices instead of a stock photo, so it's one click to buy. The seal answers "is this legit?" (licensed, local) before anyone has to ask. |
| **Rotating announcement + marquees** | The top bar cycles through four messages (licensed, made at 57 Broadway, 24/7 Bulldog, 30-day returns). A cream varsity marquee scrolls slogans; the footer marquee scrolls the residential colleges. Both pause on hover. | The key trust facts (returns, licensing, help) are repeated without taking up space. Seeing their own college name roll by gives shoppers a "this is for me" moment. |
| **Categories as felt pennants** | The five categories hang as **triangular felt pennants** on a rope, with stitching, a "from $32.00" price line, and a round product photo that spins on hover. | It's instantly collegiate, it shows price ranges before the click, and it turns the dull "browse by category" step into something people want to touch. |
| **Collections as chenille patches** | Residential Colleges / Sports / Relatives / Grad Schools as **varsity letter patches** (RC, Y, MOM, GS) with a stitched outline. | Gift buyers (parents, grandparents) spot "MOM" right away. Clear entry points for each audience lower the effort to find the right item. |
| **Product cards** | **Hang-tag prices** (punched hole, swings on hover), a dashed **stitched** inner border like an embroidered patch, a slight 3D lift and image zoom on hover, a "View details →" pill, and in-stock sizes in varsity type. | The price is the first thing the eye lands on, styled like a real tag, which builds trust. The hover motion shows each card is clickable and rewards browsing. |
| **Product page** | **Hover-to-zoom** (2× around the cursor, like picking the shirt up), a rotating "Made to order · New Haven" stamp that fades while zooming, **sizes as jersey-number tiles** (selected = filled navy; sold out = struck through in coral), and facts in a stitched cream box. | Zoom answers "what does the crest look like?" without leaving the page. Jersey tiles make the size choice (the most important step before "Add to bag") big, obvious, and on-theme. |
| **Chat feel** | The launcher is now the **Bulldog face** with "Ask the Bulldog" and wiggles on hover. A one-time **speech-bubble teaser** appears after 6 s ("Looking for your size? I check live stock"). The panel has a header with the mascot, a **green "Online" pulse**, and a white-and-coral varsity stripe. Bot messages carry the mascot avatar. The typing indicator is **three bouncing paw prints**: "checking the shelves…". | A named mascot with an "online" light feels like a shop assistant, not a form. The teaser invites a first question at the moment shoppers hesitate over sizes. The paw-print loader makes the 5–10 s wait feel intentional and on-brand. |
| **"Meet the Bulldog" band** | A home-page section showing a sample conversation (a sold-out size answered with in-stock alternatives, plus the "✓ prices checked" badge), with three one-tap example questions. | Shows what the AI can do instead of just claiming it. One tap starts a real chat, which is where the shop's biggest advantage (honest, live-stock answers) turns into sales. |
| **Motion system** | Staggered rise-in on the hero, sections that **fade up as you scroll** (IntersectionObserver), bouncy hovers on pennants and patches, a slide-in bag drawer, a pop on the bag counter, and chat bubbles that ease in. **All of it is disabled for `prefers-reduced-motion`.** | Small, purposeful motion keeps people scrolling and makes the site feel alive and well made. Respecting reduced-motion keeps it comfortable for everyone. |
| **Responsive** | On phones: logo + bag + menu on one row, full-width search below, a one-column hero with the window under the copy, and pennants and patches in 2-column grids. | About half of college-merch traffic is on phones, so the design has to hold up there. Verified at 375 px with no horizontal scrolling. |
